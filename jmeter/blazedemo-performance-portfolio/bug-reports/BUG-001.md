## What's Wrong (Summary)
Normally, a person has to search for a flight and pick one before buying it. But if you skip straight to the purchase page and send an empty request, the site doesn't stop you — it still says "success" and shows a completely made-up flight that was never chosen.

## Steps to Reproduce
1. Go to the site's home page.
2. Skip the flight search step entirely.
3. Send a purchase request straight to the purchase page with no flight information filled in.
4. Look at what the site sends back.

## What Should Happen
The site should say something went wrong (like "please search for a flight first"), since no real flight was ever picked.

## What Actually Happens
The site says the purchase worked, and shows a flight the user never picked:

```
Your flight from TLV to SFO has been reserved.
Airline: United
Flight Number: UA954
Price: 400
Arbitrary Fees and Taxes: 514.76
Total Cost: 914.76
```

None of this information matches anything the "user" actually searched for.

## Why This Matters
This means the site doesn't properly check that a purchase is connected to a real search. In a real booking system, this kind of gap could let someone "buy" things that were never really selected or priced correctly — a serious trust and data-accuracy problem.
